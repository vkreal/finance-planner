Core tracker data is user-entered via the app's own actions (no external source).

## December projection console (added Oct 2026)

Also user-entered only: year-to-date actuals, low / likely / high ranges for income still to come, ACA household / benchmark / advance-credit figures, and a strays checklist, saved as one settings blob (`december_projection_v1`) that rides the ordinary backup export / restore settings array. Pricing reuses the shipped single-year engine (`pricePlaygroundYear`) and its ACA-cliff solver (`solvePlaygroundAcaCliffFill`) from `server/src/rothOptimizer.ts` — no new tax math and no external source. On a first open the year block is preseeded from the saved Tax Plan record where available. Fixtures: the `december` suite of `runAccuracySelfCheck` (`runDecemberFixtures` in `server/src/decemberCore.ts`). December provenance (round-6, contract v53): saves write a `decemberStateFormat` marker (now 3) — a pre-marker stored beneficiary `false` STANDS as a stored answer (the Tax Plan record overlay never overrides it) and parses with `legacyBeneficiaryAnswerUnconfirmed` set, so the console asks the user to confirm it and the flag clears only when the user changes that control; a with-marker `false` is likewise genuine; missing/null still reads unanswered. Load resolution from the Tax Plan record (beneficiary fact, legacy combined-balance ownership) is a display/pricing overlay that a save never persists for a field the user has not edited in the console (`decemberStateForSave`); a legacy combined balance with no split has ownership unanswered — when the record supplies both owner amounts with a positive sum, resolution allocates the SAVED combined total across the owners by the record split's proportions (the total itself never changes; a totals mismatch over $0.01 surfaces a console note naming both totals); when the record supplies exactly one owner amount, that owner receives min(known, saved total) and the other owner receives the remainder, both as numbers, so the total is still preserved exactly (the partial-cap case — exactly one amount known and above the saved total by more than $0.01 — likewise surfaces a console note naming the record figure and the saved total), combined→primary only as the last-resort fallback when the record has no usable split (neither amount known, or a both-known sum of $0), explicit blob split always governs; legacy stray amounts predating their tax-character splits (ROC capital/ordinary, January-paid qualified portion) carry unconfirmed-character flags that drive a view note and a projection warning and change no pricing — format-3 blobs carry the flags as stored (authoritative), pre-format-3 blobs derive them (split key absent or still at its default), and each flag clears ONLY when the user edits the matching character control, never on an unrelated save. Projected tax bill (contract v50, reconciliation contract v54): each priced case also carries readouts of the same priced year at the headline draw — adjusted gross income (AGI) (the year's AGI, labeled 'Adjusted gross income (AGI)' in the bill), taxable income, state tax, and a core-computed net settle. The ACA reconciliation prices in all years against the case's EFFECTIVE final credit: the priced credit when there is one; $0 when the priced credit is null because nobody in the household can be on the marketplace on the console's Medicare facts (a known-zero credit — the allowable credit is $0 by construction); null only when the priced credit is null AND someone is marketplace-eligible but no benchmark premium was entered (genuinely unpriced). creditAtHeadline itself stays the engine's priced credit, null in both null cases; only the reconciliation uses the effective value. creditStillDue is max(0, effective final credit − APTC taken) whenever the effective credit is known; excessAptcRepayment for 2026 and later is max(0, APTC taken − effective final credit) with no repayment caps, while before 2026 it is $0 when APTC taken is at or below the effective final credit and null when APTC taken exceeds it (repayment genuinely unmodeled — pre-2026 caps are not modeled), and null in every year when the credit is genuinely unpriced. The net settle (`totalTax − creditStillDue + excessAptcRepayment`) is nullable: null only when the repayment is unmodeled (pre-2026 APTC above the effective final credit) or the credit is genuinely unpriced, so a no-marketplace year still produces a number (the total tax, plus any repayment); each null net carries a reason note (`netSettleNote`) naming which case applies — the unpriced-credit reason wins whenever the credit itself is unpriced, in any year — and the note is null whenever the net is a number; a negative net is labeled 'Estimated refund at filing' and a non-negative net 'Estimated amount to settle at filing'; before withholding / estimated payments, which the console does not track — presented as a compact bill block for the likely case under the headline (income lines behind the block's own fold; tax / credit / net always visible) and as the net line on every case card. No new tax math: every figure is a readout of the existing `pricePlaygroundYear` pricing.

## Tax Plan — tax-by-year picture (added Oct 2026)

A "Tax by year" section in the Tax Plan plan workspace, priced from the SAVED plan: it renders the baseline row of the same saved-payload compare the ending strip prices from (`runRothCompare` over `buildSavedPlanPayload`), never the working form, so an unsaved edit cannot move its figures. Contract v51 added display-only readouts to every compare scenario — `federalTaxCents` / `stateTaxCents` (the lifetime split of `totalTaxCents`) and a `years` array read straight off the priced march's rows (year, ages, federal/state tax, total income = AGI, taxable income, ACA credit kept, IRMAA paid, conversion, and pre-tax draw = conversion + pre-tax draw for spending) — no pricing change, and the published compare example gained the scalar split (its years arrays are omitted for size, as its note states). The section shows: one bar per march year of federal tax (axis scale + gridlines); a one-line composition (lifetime federal tax · ACA credits kept · lifetime IRMAA · lifetime net cost) with the score's remainder (state tax + pre-59½ penalties + tax on the ending pre-tax balance − refundable education credit) named component by component underneath so the line foots exactly in cents; per-year figures behind a fold; and a first-draw-year mini bill (adjusted gross income (AGI), taxable income, federal tax, ACA premium credit when kept, net tax = federal tax − credit; IRMAA shown only as a memo line, never as tax). The first draw year is the first march year with `pretaxDrawCents > 0`; an RMD alone does not open it, and with no draw year the block does not render.

- SALT cap for itemized deductions (T5 build, 2026-10-21): IRC §164(b)(6) as amended by §70120 of Public Law 119-21 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act). Carried as dated law-row data on each year's row in `taxLaw.ts` (saltCap / saltCapFloor / saltPhaseOutStart / saltPhaseOutRatePct), priced by the greater-of deduction step in `priceYear` (`server/src/taxEngine.ts`): each priced year deducts the greater of its standard deduction (base + §63(f) age-65+ additional amounts) and its itemized total, where the state-and-local-taxes component is first capped at the row's cap. Statutory figures: for 2025 the cap is $40,000 ($20,000 married filing separately — no MFS row exists in this engine, the same posture the T4 fixtures take), reduced by 30% of modified AGI over $500,000 but not below $10,000; for 2026–2029 the cap and the phase-down start are 101% of the prior year's ($40,400 and $505,000 for 2026), the 30% rate and $10,000 floor unchanged; for tax years after 2029 the cap reverts to $10,000 with no phase-down — a future dated row will carry that when a 2030 table is sourced (no URL is asserted for the statute text in this plan because this build ran with no web access; the citation is the Code section and the amending section of the Public Law). Modified AGI for the phase-down adds back only the §§911/931/933 foreign-income exclusions (§164(b)(6) flush language), which the engine does not model, so the phase-down runs on AGI; tax-exempt interest is NOT added back (contrast §86/§1411 MAGI). The cap figures are statutory and unindexed, so — like the NIIT threshold and the §224 senior-deduction figures — they deflate at the plan's inflation rate for each year the priced year runs past the law row it prices under; in a row's own year they apply exactly as published. The temporary senior deduction (§224, 2025–2028) is allowed whether or not the filer itemizes, so it is added on top of whichever side of the greater-of wins and is never part of the comparison; the §63(f) age-65+ additional amounts belong to the standard side only.
- Education credits (T6 build, 2026-10-21): IRC §25A — the American Opportunity Tax Credit (§25A(b)) and the Lifetime Learning Credit (§25A(c)), computed on Form 8863 (see also IRS Topic No. 313, "Education credits"). Carried as dated law-row data on each year's row in `taxLaw.ts` (aotcTier1RatePct / aotcTier1Expenses / aotcTier2RatePct / aotcTier2Expenses / aotcRefundablePct / llcRatePct / llcExpenseCap / eduPhaseOutStart / eduPhaseOutWidth), priced by `educationCreditsFor` inside `priceYear` (`server/src/taxEngine.ts`) from the household's entered per-student, per-calendar-year qualified expenses (`educationStudents`) — entered, never inferred from 529 balances, the college-years span, or any other saved field; the household's per-student aotcEligible mark is its own declaration (the engine prices credit math and phase-outs, not enrollment eligibility). Statutory figures (identical in the 2025 and 2026 rows — §25A amounts are not inflation-indexed): AOTC per eligible student = 100% of the first $2,000 of that student's qualified expenses plus 25% of the next $2,000 of those expenses (§25A(b)(1)), a $2,500 maximum per student (the §25A(b)(2) eligible-student definition is the household's to apply — the engine prices from its mark); 40% of the AOTC is refundable (§25A(i)(1)–(2); the §25A(i)(2) exceptions — a student with a felony drug conviction, and a taxpayer under 24 whose earned income is under half their support — are not separately modeled: a named limitation, with the household's eligibility mark governing). LLC = 20% of the first $10,000 of qualified expenses per RETURN (§25A(c)(1)–(2)). Coordination (§25A(g)(2) flush rule and the Form 8863 allocation): the same student's expenses cannot support both credits — the engine prices both statutory allocations (AOTC for the eligible students + LLC over the remaining students' expenses; LLC alone over all students) and takes the larger total benefit, naming the choice in every year output. Phase-out (§25A(d)): both credits reduce ratably as modified AGI runs through $160,000–$180,000 MFJ / $80,000–$90,000 otherwise; MAGI adds back only the §§911/931/933 foreign-income exclusions (§25A(d)(3)), which the engine does not model, so the phase-out runs on AGI and tax-exempt interest is NOT added back. Form 8863 rounds the phase-out ratio to three decimals; the engine uses the exact ratio, its standing treatment of statutory ratios (the §86 and §224 phase-outs price the same way). Application ordering (Form 1040): the nonrefundable portion (the AOTC remainder plus the LLC) reduces the year's income tax (ordinary + capital-gains tax) but never below $0 of income tax and never offsets the §1411 NIIT, which arrives afterward as an other tax (Schedule 2); the refundable AOTC portion is reported separately per year and lifetime and is credited in the plan score identity beside the ACA premium credit — never netted into a floored federal tax. All §25A dollar figures are statutory and unindexed, so — like the NIIT thresholds and the SALT cap — the expense caps and the phase-out range deflate at the plan's inflation rate for each year the priced year runs past the law row it prices under; in a row's own year they apply exactly as published. No URL is asserted for the statute text in this plan because this build ran with no web access; the citations are the Code sections (§25A(b), (c), (d), (g), (i)) and Form 8863 / Topic No. 313.
