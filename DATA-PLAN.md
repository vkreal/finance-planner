# Data Plan

Core tracker data is user-entered via the app's own actions (no external source).

## Retirement calculator — historical dataset (build-time, embedded)

Embedded in `client/src/retirementData.ts` (annual, 1928–2025):

- Stocks (S&P 500 total return incl. dividends) and bonds (US 10-year Treasury total return): Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern — Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills, updated Jan 5, 2026.
  Source URL (verbatim): https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html
  Spot checks against the source page: 2008 stocks −36.55%; 2022 stocks −18.04% and bonds −17.83% (both negative) — all match.
- Inflation (CPI-U annual average percent change): U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI-U (public-domain BLS series), as published in BLS CPI tables and mirrored at https://cpiinflationcalculator.com/historical-tables/ (retrieved Oct 2026). Annual-average rates used (e.g. 2008 3.8%, 2022 8.0%).

The calculator converts nominal to real returns as (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1, blends by the user's stock/bond mix, and replays every historical start year with a full plan-length sequence. The source name and URL are cited in a footnote on the calculator screen.

## Accuracy measures (added Oct 2026)

- Full dataset audit (2026-10-11): all 98 years × 3 series (294 values) in the embedded dataset (now at `server/src/retirementData.ts`, shared by the client view and the server) were compared programmatically against the published sources — stocks/bonds vs Damodaran's table as updated Jan 5, 2026 (URL above; the published page's data table mirrors the histretSP spreadsheet, whose direct .xls link returned 404 to the fetcher), CPI vs the BLS CPI-U annual-average percent changes as published in the BLS CPI-U table (monthly values and Avg–Avg percent changes, mirrored at https://www.coinnews.net/tools/cpi-inflation-calculator/consumer-price-index-cpi-and-annual-percent-changes-from-1913-to-2008/ and cross-checked against the BLS percent-change table at https://files.snappages.site/j4hr5rfob1/assets/files/Inflation.pdf). Result: 0 discrepancies at rounding tolerance (|diff| ≤ 0.011); no values changed.
- Regression fixtures: `server/src/retirementFixtures.ts` pins 40 cases (Projection / Historical / Monte Carlo × Constant / Percent / Guardrails incl. non-default knobs, extra-income mid-plan and surplus cases, the Max Spending solver per strategy × engine, and at-risk boundary cases just above/below the 95% Historical and 85% Monte Carlo cutoffs), runnable via the `runAccuracySelfCheck` maintenance action.
- Trinity benchmark anchor (same action): $1,000,000, retire immediately, 30 years, $40,000/yr constant real spending. Published comparison: Cooley, Hubbard & Walz (1998), the Trinity Study, Table 3 (inflation-adjusted, 30-year, 4%): 50/50 = 95%, 75/25 = 100% (1926–1995 Ibbotson data, corporate bonds) — as analysed at https://retirementresearcher.com/safe-withdrawal-rates-for-retirement-and-the-trinity-study/ and summarised at https://keepthrifty.com/ (search-surfaced summary). App rates (1928–2025 window, 69 windows, 10-year Treasury blend): computed by the self-check at runtime.
- Data integrity checks (same action + on every getDashboard / getNetWorth load): NW snapshot totals, NW bucket splits, bill version chains, payment references, cash-out items. Violations surface as warning lines in the relevant view only when any exist.

The engines live in `server/src/retirementEngines.ts`, imported by the client via `@space/server/retirementEngines` and by the server actions directly — one implementation for the UI and the self-check.

## Roth Conversion view — tax-law table (build-time, embedded)

- Federal brackets, standard deduction, age-65+ add-ons, LTCG thresholds — 2026: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (std. deduction $32,200 MFJ / $16,100 single / $24,150 HOH; MFJ bracket tops 24,800 / 100,800 / 211,400 / 403,550 / 512,450 / 768,700; HOH 24% / 32% bracket tops $201,750 / $256,200).

  Spot checks against the sources: 2026 standard deductions and the 37% tops ($768,700 MFJ / $640,600 single) match the table in `taxLaw.ts`.
- Federal figures — 2025: IRS Rev.   Primary source (IRS, IR-2024-273 announcing Rev. Proc. 2024-40): https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2025

- IRMAA tiers and Part B / Part D amounts — 2026 premiums: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services fact sheet, November 14, 2025 (standard Part B $202.90/mo; MFJ thresholds 218k / 274k / 342k / 410k / 750k; Tier 1 couple cost with Part D = $2,296.80/yr). 2025 premiums per the CMS November 2024 release (standard Part B $185.00).

- ACA applicable-percentage tables: 2026 — IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-25 (9.96% at 300–400% FPL; credit $0 above 400% FPL — the pre-2026 cliff is back); 2025 table per Rev. Proc. 2024-35 / the enhanced-credit schedule in effect for 2025.
  Primary source (IRS, Rev. Proc. 2025-25): https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-25.pdf
  Rev. Proc. 2025-25 as summarized by Current Federal Tax Developments: https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/7/18/rev-proc-2025-25-a-technical-review-of-2026-premium-tax-credit-and-affordability-adjustments

- HHS poverty guidelines (contiguous US): 2026 guidelines — $15,960 for a household of 1 + $5,680 per additional person (household of 2 = $21,640, so the 2027-coverage ACA cliff is $86,560; household of 5 = $38,680); 2025 guidelines — $15,650 + $5,500 (household of 2 = $21,150, so the 2026-coverage ACA cliff is $84,600); 2024 guidelines — $15,060 + $5,380. An ACA coverage year uses the PRIOR year's guidelines. Guideline years beyond the newest row (2026) fall back to that newest (2026) row — `fplFor` in `taxLaw.ts` resolves guideline 2027+ to the 2026 guidelines, so coverage 2028+ prices on the 2026 guidelines (the table's today's-dollars convention: future years reuse the newest row).
  Source URLs (verbatim): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-01300/annual-update-of-the-hhs-poverty-guidelines and https://aspe.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/documents/dd73d4f00d8a819d10b2fdb70d254f7b/detailed-guidelines-2025.pdf
- RMD divisors: Uniform Lifetime Table and Joint Life and Last Survivor Table (Table II), IRS Publication 590-B; RMD ages per SECURE 2.0 (born 1951–1959 → 73, born 1960+ → 75; historical: born 1949–1950 → 72, born 1948 or earlier → 70½).
  Primary source (IRS, RMD topics / Publication 590-B): https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-required-minimum-distributions-rmds

Reg. §1.401(a)(9)-9(d), as updated by T.D. 9930 for distribution years 2022 and later — transcribed in full into `taxLaw.ts` (owner ages 70–120 × spouse ages 20–120, every cell). (The earlier partial grid was extracted from an IRS RMD worksheet PDF; that worksheet reproduces the PRE-2022 table and is no longer the source.) Transcription validation: all 176 cells of the previous partial grid match the published values exactly; the published table is symmetric in the two ages and the transcription has zero asymmetries and zero monotonicity violations.
  Spot checks: Uniform divisor at 75 = 24.6; Joint Life owner 80 / spouse 70 = 20.2, owner 90 / spouse 80 = 12.2 — all match the publication, and the 80 / 70 cell is pinned in the tax fixtures (tax-rmd-divisors).
- Gift-tax annual exclusion and kiddie-tax threshold (used only by the Gift stock what-if, `server/src/giftModel.ts`): the §2503(b) annual exclusion is $19,000 per donor per recipient in both 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2024-40) and 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, which states the exclusion "remains at $19,000"). The kiddie-tax threshold (IRC §1(g)) is twice the amount in effect under §63(c)(5)(A) — $1,350 in both 2025 and 2026 — so the threshold is $2,700 in both years; later years fall back to the 2026 row like the other constants. Kiddie-tax construction (E3 build, 2026-10-19): when the gate fires, the child's gain up to the threshold prices on the child's own return and the excess prices as the parents' Form 8615 allocable tax — the with-minus-without difference of stacking the excess onto the parents' own sale-year gains staircase (Form 8615, Tax for Certain Children Who Have Unearned Income, implementing IRC §1(g)) — so the excess fills the parents' remaining 0% gains space before pricing at 15%/20%. (Pre-E3 the excess was charged at the parents' single top marginal rate flat.)
  Primary source for the 2026 exclusion (IRS, IR-2025-103 announcing Rev. Proc. 2025-32): https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
